### Version B — Architect / Contractor Design Brief

**Project:** Owner-built primary residence, Steuben County area, New York  
**Program:** Full-time off-grid homestead, owner-occupant with some DIY labor

#### Building Envelope
- **Footprint:** 24 ft × 50 ft (1,200 sq ft per floor)
- **Stories:** Two full floors
- **Roof:** Shed roof, single slope, high side at rear/north, low side at front/south. Standing seam metal roofing, dark finish. Solar array integrated on rear roof slope.
- **Structure:** Heavy timber frame, post-and-beam, exposed interior. Straw bale infill panels between structural bays.
- **Exterior finish:** Three-coat lime stucco, spray-applied base coats, hand-finished top coat. Pigmented with on-site clay/soil for site-responsive color. Warm ochre-to-sienna palette expected.
- **Interior finish:** Lime plaster walls throughout, matching exterior palette. Exposed timber frame elements left natural or lightly oiled.

#### South Facade — Passive Solar + Greenhouse
- Full-width attached greenhouse on south-facing front facade, approximately 24 ft wide × 10–12 ft deep
- Greenhouse glazing: double-wall polycarbonate or low-e glass panels in timber or steel subframe
- Greenhouse serves as passive solar thermal buffer, food growing space, and main entry
- Thermal mass wall (Trombe wall or exposed masonry) behind greenhouse to absorb and re-radiate heat

#### Masonry Heater — Central Core
- Central masonry heater positioned at or near the geometric center of the ground floor
- Functions as: primary whole-house heat source, staircase core (stairs wrap around the mass), thermal anchor of the building
- Exterior: smooth lime stucco finish, warm cream, monolithic form
- Includes integrated heat exchanger coil for radiant floor water loop
- Style reference: Russian/Finnish masonry heater tradition (Grundofen, Kachelofen, or rocket mass heater variant)
- Approximate footprint: 4 ft × 6 ft base, rising full two-story height
- Staircase wraps the heater mass from ground floor to second floor

#### Mechanical Systems
- **Radiant floor heating:** Closed-loop hydronic system fed by masonry heater heat exchanger. PEX tubing embedded in slab (Scenario A) or suspended below subfloor in sleepers (Scenario B). Single zone, ground floor.
- **Solar:** Off-grid or hybrid grid-tied system. 5–8 kW array on rear roof slope. Battery bank (LiFePO4 preferred). Roof pitch optimized for 42°N latitude (Steuben County).
- **Water heating:** Electric on-demand tankless water heater, sized for off-grid solar load.
- **Greywater:** Constructed wetland or branched drain gravity system. Laundry and sink water recaptured for subsurface irrigation or toilet flushing.
- **Septic:** Site-dependent. Prefer connection to existing system if purchasing improved property.

#### Foundation — Two Scenarios (see cost section)
- **Scenario A:** Poured concrete slab on grade, 4" reinforced, rigid insulation below (R-10 min), perimeter frost wall to 48" depth (NY frost line). Radiant PEX embedded in slab pour.
- **Scenario B:** Helical or poured concrete piers, pressure-treated timber floor framing, raised deck. Radiant in suspended subfloor sleeper system. Preferred on sloped sites or where existing basement is present.

## PART 2 — COST BREAKDOWN

### Important Notes Before You Read These Numbers

1. **These are realistic estimates, not guarantees.** Prices vary significantly in rural NY, and natural building contractors are rare — scarcity drives cost up.
2. **Your $120,000 total budget (land + build) is very tight for this full spec.** The honest range is $140,000–$240,000+ for the complete build. See the phasing strategy at the bottom to get to move-in condition within budget.
3. **Your owner labor matters.** Estimated savings from your direct involvement: **$15,000–$28,000**.
4. **Solar and masonry heater are your two largest wild-card costs.** Both have wide ranges depending on approach.

### How to Close the Gap — Phasing Strategy

You don't have to build everything at once. Here's a phased approach that gets you to **move-in condition for ~$110,000–$130,000** and adds systems over time:

#### Phase 1 — Move-In Ready (~$95,000–$125,000)
Include: Land, site prep, foundation, timber frame, straw bale, stucco, metal roof, masonry heater, windows, basic electrical, basic plumbing, kitchen/bath fixtures, greenhouse structure (glazed later if needed), permits.

*Defer:* Full solar, battery bank, greywater, radiant floor (use masonry heater only for heat Phase 1).

#### Phase 2 — Within 1–2 Years (~$18,000–$30,000)
Add: Full solar array + battery bank (federal tax credit still applies), radiant floor system (add heat exchanger to masonry heater), greywater branched drain system.

#### Biggest Savings Levers
- **Build the masonry heater yourself** with a kit or cob/brick construction guidance: saves $8,000–$20,000
- **Source timber locally** from a Steuben/Allegany County sawyer: saves $5,000–$15,000
- **Polycarbonate greenhouse** instead of glass: saves $4,000–$8,000
- **Phase solar** to a starter 3 kW system: saves $6,000–$10,000 upfront
- **Your labor** on stucco, bale stacking, carpentry: saves $15,000–$28,000

### Key Contractors to Find in Your Region

- **Timber frame:** Finger Lakes Timber Frames (Ithaca area), or search Timber Framers Guild directory for NY members
- **Natural building / straw bale:** The Canelo Project and strawbale.com maintain contractor directories; rare in NY but exist
- **Masonry heater:** Masonry Heater Association of North America (mha-net.org) — certified heater masons
- **Stucco spray:** Any EIFS/stucco contractor can spray base coats; lime finish is specialty — ask for lime plaster experience
- **Solar:** NYSERDA has a certified contractor list; focus on off-grid experienced installers
